# Lumetry

A hex-grid puzzle game about the messy beauty of experimental science. You play [Tess](#lumetry/characters/tess.md), a PhD student turned loose in a research facility full of eccentric professors and machines built the week before the safety inspection. Every level is a self-contained lab room: aim mirrors, prisms, and strange hardware to connect a laser into a [measurement device](#lumetry/mechanics/detectors.md) and unlock the next chamber. Each room layers a new idea over the last, so plain reflection grows into multi-step optics without the player noticing the ramp.

Short, snappy rooms, one puzzle each. 18 [experiments](#lumetry/gameplay/overview.md), six wings of three (one per faculty member), threaded with the faculty bickering between chambers, ~60 minutes end to end. Clearing each wing pops one of six [achievements](#lumetry/gameplay/achievements.md). Available on Steam.

The vibe is science by way of Saturday-morning cartoon: a little absurd, a little brilliant, proudly over-engineered, loud with sizzling lasers and handwritten notes, always one step away from combustion. See [art direction](#lumetry/art-direction.md).

## Naming Conventions

Every professor is named for a unit of measurement, and the unit fits the person. Newton is force, Radian is angle, Candela and Lumen and Lux are three different flavors of light, Joule is raw energy. Each one hands the player exactly one new piece of hardware, then gets out of the way. [Tess](#lumetry/characters/tess.md) is short for Tesla, the unit of magnetism because she pulls everything together.
