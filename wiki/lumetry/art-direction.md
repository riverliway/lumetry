# Art Direction

Somewhere between 90s cartoons and anime, tilted hard toward the 90s side: unrealistic proportions, visual gags, sharp angles, palettes that shout. The vibe is *Helltaker* crossed with *Panty & Stocking*, dressed in the science-set decor of *Dexter's Laboratory* and *Johnny Test*.

## The Cast

Fully rendered waist-up portraits carry the dialogue, at the top of each [experiment](#lumetry/gameplay/overview.md) and on trigger events mid-puzzle. The same portraits do double duty as Steam box-art, website art, and the little marketing there is. [Tess](#lumetry/characters/tess.md)'s in-game body is a simplified chibi of her portrait.

The important art for this game will be the waist-up fully rendered portraits that accompany dialogue boxes that start off puzzles or pop up after a specific trigger event mid-puzzle. Outside of the game, fully rendered portraits will also be used in the “box-art” on the steam page, on the website for the game, and in any marketing materials. The controllable character model of Tess in the game will be a simplified/chibi version of her fully rendered portrait.

![Hades dialogue-box portrait, format reference](/lumetry/references/dialogue-portrait-hades.png)

The hard rule, learned from squinting at *Helltaker* and *Awaria*: the cast has to be legible at a glance. Take off your glasses, step back, and you should still tell everyone apart. Thus no shared palette and no shared silhouette. Each faculty member gets a distinct dominant color and a shape that ties back to their personality and their mechanic. [Newton](#lumetry/characters/prof-newton.md) is mass and force, [Radian](#lumetry/characters/prof-radian.md) is angles, [Candela](#lumetry/characters/prof-candela.md) dims, [Lumen](#lumetry/characters/prof-lumen.md) is all sparkle, and [Joule](#lumetry/characters/prof-joule.md) is explosive.

## Palette

Blue-lab base, vivid accent pops, saturated character colors kept far enough apart on the wheel to read across a room.

## Reference board

I really liked Helltaker with its style somewhere between 90’s cartoons and anime. I think I want to learn a bit further into the 90s cartoons style. Unrealistic proportions, visual gags, vivid palettes, etc. The only issue is that I feel like a lot of the characters in Helltaker look a bit same-y since they have the same color palette and similar outfits.. Like if I took off my glasses and squinted from a distance, I want to be able to tell the characters apart in the cast.

![Helltaker style reference](/lumetry/references/style-helltaker.png)

Awaria is done by the same artist. Love the style, but having a uniform color for the ghosts is very challenging to see zoomed out.

![Awaria style reference](/lumetry/references/style-awaria.png)

I stumbled across this fanart of hazbin hotel on Instagram and I love the art style. It’s the perfect blend of simple lines and colors but also includes good shading. I love the proportions of the character - unrealistic but not too ridiculous. I think this balance of cartoony-anime would be perfect for what I’m looking for, but the colors are too drab, I want more vivid pops of color.

![Hazbin Hotel fanart style reference](/lumetry/references/style-hazbin-fanart.jpg)

I saw the first episode of Panty & Stocking recently and I really liked their artstyle. The simplistic but well designed outfits, the distinctive eyes that draw you in, expressive faces, the vivid colors, all great. I like how they’re willing to do unrealistic proportions, but I’m not as much of a fan of the really wide legs, it makes me feel like I’m short and looking up at the character.

![Panty and Stocking style reference](/lumetry/references/style-panty-and-stocking.png)

When I was first thinking of cartoon characters and scientists, Johnny Test popped up because I watched a lot of it as a kid. I think the character designs are a little mundane, they’re a bit too simple, but I like the sharp angles and proportions. I saw in your preliminary designs you had already given each cast member a “shape” that relates back to their personality and game mechanic. That is something I think Johnny Test does as well, at least with the 3 kids.

![Johnny Test style reference](/lumetry/references/style-johnny-test.png)

When I think about what the science of this world looks like, Dexter’s Laboratory comes to mind. The character designs in this show are a little too unrealistic for my taste, but I like the science aesthetics. A lot of blues with vivid pops of color for the random buttons, screens, chemicals, etc. Things are chaotically organized, but pretty clean overall. There isn’t grime and trash and scorch marks all over the machinery. All sorts of modern physics and technology are on the table, but trying not to step too far over the bound into science fiction.
